Buying guide

How to choose a wireless charger: Qi or Qi2? Is a 3-in-1 right for you?

Before you buy a 3-in-1 wireless station, check four things: the charging standard your phone supports, your watch type, the power adapter, and your case thickness. This guide keeps it simple.

Qi or Qi2?

Qi is the common older standard: put the phone on the pad and it charges, but placement must be just right.

Qi2 is newer: magnets snap the phone into the right spot, with up to 15 W. Recent iPhones and a growing number of Android phones support it.

If your phone has no magnetic charging, a magnetic charger needs a magnetic case or stick-on ring to hold it.

Check your watch before buying a 3-in-1

Most 3-in-1 stations are built for Apple Watch, or include a puck for specific Samsung watches.

Many other watches use their own cable and will not work. Read the compatibility list on the product page first.

The adapter: the #1 cause of slow charging

Many stations ship with a cable only and need a fast PD adapter of 20 W or more, as stated by the seller.

With a weak adapter they charge slowly or drop one of the devices.

Case and heat: realistic expectations

  • Thick cases (over roughly 5 mm) or cases with cards or metal weaken or block charging.
  • Wireless charging is slower than a cable and the phone gets a little warm; that is normal.
  • The real benefit is comfort and a tidy desk: drop your devices at night and forget the cables.

What we avoid when choosing

  • Inflated wattage your phone cannot even use.
  • Products without a clear watch compatibility list.
  • Ratings under 85%.

Frequently asked questions

Does wireless charging damage the battery?

Not meaningfully with a good charger. Excess heat is what hurts, so avoid charging under a pillow or in the sun.

Does it work with any phone?

Only with phones that support Qi or Qi2 wireless charging. Check your phone specs.

Why does charging stop sometimes?

Usually a weak adapter, a thick case, or the phone being off-center on the pad.

Do I need a 15 W charger?

Only if your phone supports it; otherwise it charges at your phone's own maximum.
