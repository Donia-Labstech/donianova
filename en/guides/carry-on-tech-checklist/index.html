Carry-on tech checklist for travel

A practical list of what to pack in your carry-on before any trip, by plane or by car, with the battery rules you should know at the airport.

Home/Carry-on tech checklist

Last updated: 2026-11-13

The essentials for any trip

  • A universal travel adapter, because plug shapes differ between countries.
  • A power bank and a short charging cable.
  • A tracker tag inside checked luggage, so you know where it is if it is delayed.
  • An RFID blocking card for your cards and passport at the airport and in crowds.

Battery rules on planes

The general rule with most airlines: power banks go in carry-on only, never in checked luggage, and batteries up to 100 Wh are usually allowed without special approval.

A 10,000 mAh power bank is about 37 Wh, well within the limit. Always check your airline's rules before you fly.

Travel adapters: what to look for

  • Covers European, UK, US and Australian sockets.
  • Has USB-C and USB-A ports to charge a phone and earbuds together.
  • An adapter does not convert voltage, so check that your charger supports 100–240 V (most phone chargers do).

For road trips

An OBD2 scanner reads engine fault codes before you set off, so you know whether a check-engine light is a minor warning or needs a mechanic. It also helps when buying a used car.

For outings in nature, a pocket monocular adds fun for birdwatching and distant views, with realistic zoom expectations.

One last tip

Keep every cable and the adapter in one small pouch. Security checks go faster and you will not leave anything in the hotel.

Frequently asked questions

Can a power bank go in checked luggage?

No. Most airlines require it in your carry-on.

Does an adapter convert voltage?

No, it only changes the plug shape. Make sure your charger supports 100–240 V.

Do tracker tags work everywhere?

They rely on Bluetooth and nearby phones, so they work best in airports and cities.
